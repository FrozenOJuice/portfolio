## Problem and solution

A calendar reserves time; a workout log records what happened. Treating a planned
workout as a completed session makes history unreliable.

Every168 connects weekly planning with detailed workout logging while keeping
reusable workouts, scheduled snapshots, and actual results separate. Completing
a session updates its recorded calendar time without duplicating it.

## My role

I developed the Calendar and Fitness workflows across the Next.js interface,
TypeScript API, and database. My focus included safe writes, account isolation,
timezone correctness, and preserving workout history through later edits.

## Technologies and architecture

Next.js and React use a typed API client backed by shared Zod contracts. An Express
modular monolith separates routes, domain services, and repositories, with Drizzle
and PostgreSQL for persistence. Cross-domain work goes through public services;
import-boundary checks enforce that separation.

## Engineering challenges

### Recovering interrupted writes

A committed save can lose its response. Idempotency receipts commit with the write,
while expected revisions reject stale edits. The browser preserves the exact
pending request for a safe retry instead of creating a second record.

### Handling local time correctly

Calendar boundaries follow the account's timezone and week-start preference.
Daylight-saving transitions can produce 167- or 169-hour weeks. Editing unchanged
local fields preserves their original timestamps through repeated-hour boundaries.

### Keeping history trustworthy

Finished sessions retain their captured exercise definitions independently of
reusable workouts. Corrections and deletions recalculate derived records, and
comparison reads use a consistent database snapshot during concurrent changes.

## Results, testing, and limitations

The October 30, 2026 remediation report records passing unit, architecture,
PostgreSQL integration, and browser suites. Coverage includes lost-response
recovery, concurrent corrections, account isolation, keyboard access, and DST
boundaries; [the report](https://github.com/FrozenOJuice/Every168/blob/main/docs/FIRST_RELEASE_REMEDIATION.md)
records the scope and follow-up checks.

Every168 is demonstrated locally, without a hosted service. Write recovery is
limited to the current browser tab; offline synchronization and external calendar
sync are outside the presented scope.
